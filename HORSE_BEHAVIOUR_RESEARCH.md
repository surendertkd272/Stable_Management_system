## 1. The five things that matter most

1. **Each horse's own normal beats any textbook range.**
   - Healthy horses differ enormously. Lying time runs from 0 to 319 minutes a day between horses, and snort rate differs about 17-fold.
   - The same horse also varies night to night: by about 47–77 minutes a day in standing and eating time.
   - So:
     - never alarm on one night;
     - compare each horse with its own last 7–14 nights;
     - alert when two or more measures move together.
2. **Colic is usually quiet at first.**
   - The commonest early picture is *eating less and passing fewer droppings*, often without rolling, pawing or flank-watching.
   - Two signs point to a critical case:
     - **severe sweating:** odds 20.7× (1,016 cases, Curtis 2015);
     - **repeated attempts to lie down:** odds 12×.
3. **Horses hide pain from people.**
   - Discomfort behaviours fall by 77% when a caretaker is at the stall, and in 30% of horses they stop completely (Torcivia & McDonnell 2020).
   - Unwatched video sees what a stable visit does not. This is the strongest argument for a stall camera.
4. **Eye temperature is a trend, never a fever reading.**
   - Published offsets from rectal temperature range from 2 °C *below* to 3–5 °C *above*. The gap depends on the camera and distance, and the error grows beyond 1 m.
   - Month, farm and breed shift it by up to 2.4 °C.
   - Field readings did not track rectal temperature at all, and an eye thermometer caught only half of fevers.
   - It is still useful against the horse's own baseline, at the same hour, out of the sun and not after work. Strangles shows a fever 24–48 h before other signs.
5. **Heat is the biggest seasonal risk in India.** The early signs are faster breathing, sweating and standing near water. Eating less comes late. A horse that cannot sweat (anhidrosis, 12% in Lahore) is a real risk that thermal imaging could screen for.

## 2. Rest and sleep

| Pattern | Normal | Warning | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lying per day | Wide range: 25 ± 22 min (accelerometers), 67 ± 62 min (0–319; 83 horses), **90 min in 80 stalled cavalry horses**, 135–145 min on video in single boxes | Far below the horse's own level for several nights | Gobbo 2025, Kelemen 2021, Ogilvie-Graham 1994, Kjellberg 2022, Raabymagle 2006 |
| Bouts | 1–7 a night, 10–40 min each; the longest 00:00–04:00; night rest about 21:00–06:00 | Many short, interrupted bouts (colic) | Kelemen 2021, Torcivia 2021 |
| Lying flat on the side | About a third of lying; single spells 5–7 min | Flat and *awake*: head lifting, legs moving (pain) | Raabymagle 2006, Maskato 2020 |
| Deep (REM) sleep | About **30 min a night** (22–37). Happens only lying down: flat, or on the chest with the head on the ground | No REM-capable lying for several nights | Zimmer 2026, Burla 2017, Aleman 2008 |
| Standing rest | Most rest is standing; about 4.5 h a day resting a hind leg; both hinds used about equally | Always the same hind (62:38 in hind-lame horses); a pointed **front** leg | Ogilvie-Graham 1994, Uellendahl 2024 |
| Getting up | About 4 get-ups a night, at the first attempt; a half-roll before rising in 12–33% | Several attempts, a long sitting phase, more rolling to rise | Raabymagle 2006 |
| **Sleep deprivation** | — | **Buckling or collapsing while dozing.** Up to 199 times a day, 87% in REM while standing, mostly at night; knees injured in 72% of horses, fetlocks in 68%. Signs appear after about 10 days to 4 weeks | Fuchs 2017, Aleman 2008 |
| New place | Lying and REM are suppressed for nights to weeks; some horses do not lie down at all for a few days | Still no lying after about 2 weeks, or collapses | Zimmer 2026, Oliveira 2022, Greening 2018 |

**What changes the picture:**
- Straw gives 3× more flat-out lying than shavings.
- A bigger box gives longer flat-out spells.
- Lights on all night and thin bedding mean less lying.
- Moving a horse into a single stall disturbed its behaviour for a week and changed its immune cells (Schmucker 2022).

**Disagreement:** whether age or lameness changes lying time.
- Kelemen 2021 (83 horses) found no effect.
- Other studies found that younger horses lie more, and that hind-lame horses lie more at night.

**Correction to what EquiCare had:**
- **Normal lying:** we had "25–67 min a day, 2–4 bouts of 10–16 min". That was too narrow and too low.
- **REM sleep:** we had "about 20 min, flat only". It is about 30 min, and also happens on the chest.
- **"At least 30 min of lying a day":** this is an *assumption* in the literature, not a measured line. Collapses are the deciding sign.

## 3. Pain, colic and lameness

**Colic, from 1,016 first-opinion cases** (Curtis 2015):

| Sign | How often seen in colic | Odds of a critical case |
|---|---|---|
| Attempts to lie down | 77% | **12×** when severe |
| Flank watching | 68% | — |
| Pawing | 60% | — |
| Sweating | 44% | **20.7×** when severe |
| Kicking at the belly | 43% | — |

- Head lowered in 18%, agitated in 32%. 24% of cases were critical.
- Only 9% of owners felt sure they would recognise mild colic (Bowden 2019).

**Laminitis:**
- The classic rocked-back stance was seen in **fewer than 25%** of 577 cases (Wylie).
- Over 70% showed difficulty turning, a stilted walk or lameness at walk.
- Look for reluctance to turn or move, and more lying.

**Pain scales and what a door camera can use:**
- **Horse Grimace Scale:** 6 face items, scored 0–2. Observers agree best on ears (ICC 0.97) and least on nostrils (0.58).
- **Equine Pain Face; EQUUS-COMPASS / FAP for colic:** sensitivity 77–96%, specificity 71–100%.
- **The limit:** all of these were validated on close-up faces. A whole-stall door view gives the face too few pixels.
- **Body signs that do work at stall distance:**
  - position in the box;
  - head height;
  - weight shifting: 13 an hour in severe pain against 2 an hour pain-free (Nowak 2024);
  - eating bouts: 2 an hour in pain against 5;
  - restless lying.
- **Automatic video pain recognition:** reached F1 about 73% against 55% for vets (Broomé 2019), but fell on horses the model had not seen.

**Other conditions to recognise:**

| Condition | What it looks like | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Tying-up | After work: camped out, sweating, refuses to move | **Vet now** |
| Pleuropneumonia | Within a week of transport: dull, fever, stands braced with elbows out, shallow fast breathing | **Vet now.** Relevant to army horse moves |
| Dental pain | Dropping balls of chewed hay, slow eating | Dentist |
| Eye ulcer | Squinting, tears | **Vet now.** Can perforate within 12 h |
| Tetanus | "Sawhorse" stance, stiff tail | **Vet now** |
| Gastric ulcers | Cannot be told from a camera: only 1 of 52 ulcer horses crib-bit, and most signs showed under saddle | — |

**Withdrawn posture** (Fureix 2012):
- **What it looks like:** neck level with the back, eyes open and staring, ears back, no movement. Bouts last 17–97 s.
- **Meaning:** a depression-like state in 24% of riding-school horses. It may reveal chronic pain.
- **Don't confuse with dozing:** a dozing horse has its eyes half closed, lip loose and a hind leg rested.

## 4. Stable vices and welfare

| Vice | Share of horses | When | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crib-biting | 2.4–8.3% (13% of Thoroughbreds); about 1,500 bites a day | After concentrate | **Epiploic foramen entrapment colic, odds 34.7×**. Ulcer link in foals, not adults |
| Weaving | About 3% | Before the morning feed and before turnout | Confinement, little contact. Blocking it moves it further into the stall |
| Box walking | About 2% | — | Confinement; separation if only when a neighbour leaves |
| Head tossing | — | — | Could be **trigeminal headshaking** (nerve pain, about 1%): sunlight is the trigger in 61% of cases. A vet should see it, especially in a sunny posting |
| Self-biting | Up to 2% of stallions | — | Injury risk: vet |

- **How often vices are seen depends on how you look:**
  - 1–2% on short farm visits;
  - 86% of boxed sport horses once minor oral habits count;
  - continuous video will see more than inspections do.
- **Is a vice coping or distress?** Unclear: 7 of 21 cortisol studies found higher cortisol, 6 found lower. Treat a vice as a *management flag* (forage, contact, exercise), not proof of distress now. Never recommend collars or grilles as the fix.
- **Build-up before feeds:**
  - Stereotypies rise from 2.2 to 7.2 events in the last 20 min before a feed.
  - Horses with hay always available show no build-up, so a build-up is itself a sign of restricted forage.
  - A feed one hour late increased pawing and door kicking.

**Signs of good and poor welfare:**

| | What it looks like | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Calm exploring, ears and neck scanning, relaxed lower lip, lying down | — |
| Good | **Snorts**: 7–13 an hour; fewer with chronic stress | Needs audio |
| Poor | Withdrawn posture; ears back most of the time (≥50% of checks) | — |
| Poor | Facing the wall | — |
| Poor | Aggression at the door | — |
| Poor | Standing fixed and still in more than 15% of checks | — |

**Neighbours matter:**
- Horses kept without contact had higher stress hormones and **higher eye temperature** (Yarnell 2015, small study).
- Being able to touch a neighbour reduced abnormal behaviour more than sight alone did. A mirror reduced weaving.

## 5. Eating, drinking, droppings, urine

| Measure | Normal | Warning | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meal time | About 33 min per kg of hay, 12–16 min per kg of concentrate; hay nets slow it | Meal much longer than the horse's own (ours: 1.5×), feed left | Scheurer 2017, Ellis 2015 |
| Eating per day | 3–4 h on set rations; 8.7 h in stalled cavalry horses; 8–14 h grazing | A drop against the horse's own level; fewer eating bouts | Ogilvie-Graham 1994, Nowak 2024 |
| Night gap without forage | — | More than 4 h in 74% of stabled horses on shavings (ulcer and colic risk) | Baumgartner 2020 |
| Water | At least 5 L per 100 kg a day (21–29 L for 500 kg on a mixed ration; over 70 L after work in heat); 25–70 mL/kg | Near zero for a day (impaction colic); over 100 mL/kg (kidney disease, PPID) | Merck, Barrell 2022 |
| Drinks per day | About 13 a day at 30–35 °C. Least 05:00–09:00, most 13:00–17:00. More and longer drinks when dehydrated | — | Crowell-Davis 1985, Pritchard 2008 |
| Water source | Buckets beat slow-filling bowls (58 vs 43 mL/kg a day) | — | Nyman 2001 |
| Droppings | 4–15 a day; drier and fewer when stabled | Fewer droppings with eating less: **the commonest early colic sign**. First 5 days after a feed or housing change | REACT, Williams 2015 |
| Urine | About 5 a day (4–15); 15–30 mL/kg a day | Frequent small urinations, straining (stones, cystitis); stance with no stream (colic); **red or brown urine: piroplasmosis in India** | Sambraus 2003, Merck, Maharana 2026 |
| Rolling | After work and baths, to cool down; a half-roll before rising | Repeated, or with pain signs | Luz 2021 |

**Correction:** a camera can count visits to the water but cannot measure litres. Litres need a flow meter.

## 6. Heat (important for RVC)

- **Effect of sun at 30.6 °C** (Holcomb 2013):

  | | In shade | In sun |
  |---|---|---|
  | Breathing (breaths/min) | 20.5 | 25.5 |
  | Sweating (share of checks) | 1% | 51% |
  | Time near water | 20% | 34% |

  Body changes came first, at 12:30; behaviour changed later. Eating did not change.
- **Heat stress in working equids:** fast, deep breathing, flared nostrils, head nodding and apathy go with a higher rectal temperature (Pritchard 2006).
- **Anhidrosis (cannot sweat):**
  - **How common:** 12.4% of 290 horses in Lahore (peak July); 2% in Florida; 6–25% of Florida Thoroughbreds over a career. No Indian study exists.
  - **Signs:** fast breathing that does not settle, a dry coat, hair loss on the face, chest or croup.
  - **Screening idea (not yet tested):** a hot, dry coat on thermal imaging after work.
- **Heat stroke:** 41–43 °C, dull, weak. **Cool now and call the vet.**
- **Heat index:** the vets' rule adds °F and % humidity. Under 130 is fine, 130–150 means monitor, 150–180 is critical. A 34 °C day at 50–60% humidity scores **143–153**. On hot days, judge breathing and eye temperature against *hot-day* levels.

## 7. India and military context

- **Glanders:** notifiable. About 60% of positives come from Uttar Pradesh. **Army equines have historically stayed free** under strict surveillance (ICAR-NRCE).
- **Surra** (blood parasite): about 5% of equines in north India, **peaking after the monsoon (Sep–Nov)**.
- **Piroplasmosis:** common in Rajasthan. Signs are red urine and fever.
- **Equine influenza:** spread across 11 or more states in 2008–09.
- **What behaviour can do:** it can flag a horse for a blood test (cough, dullness, eating less, weight loss). It cannot diagnose these diseases.
- **Working equids in India:**
  - Over 90% have hoof or limb problems and about 85% are thin, more so in warmer climates.
  - Brooke's welfare tool, used in India, scores attitude as alert, apathetic or severely depressed.
- **Transport and moves:**
  - pleuropneumonia risk within a week of a long journey;
  - behaviour unsettled for 1–2 weeks after a stall move;
  - horses drink little in hot transport.
- **Animal types:** mules need their own reference values.
- **No behaviour data exist for Marwari, Kathiawari or Indian country-bred horses.** RVC's data would be the first.
- **The only military dataset:** the Household Cavalry (80 stalled horses, 5,424 hours of video). They lay down 1.5 h a day, rested a hind leg 4.5 h a day and ate 8.7 h a day.

## 8. Body language and sounds

- **Ears:**
  - They show where the horse's attention is (Wathan & McComb 2014).
  - Ears back most of the time goes with poor welfare and back problems (one conference study).
- **Stress face:** more eye white, wide nostrils and ear flicking. These **overlap the pain face**, so stress (transport, isolation) can trigger a false "pain" flag (Lundblad 2021).
- **Sounds a microphone could add:**
  - **Long whinnies:** separation stress (Briefer 2015).
  - **Snorts:** a positive sign (Stomp 2018).
  - **Groaning or teeth grinding:** with lying or rolling, discomfort.
  - **Coughs:** counts by audio matched video over 97% of the time. A cough cluster across stalls needs the vet.
- **Mare in season:**
  - Frequent squatting urination, tail raised, squealing, for 5–7 days about every 21 days.
  - It looks like a urinary or colic sign, so it should be labelled to avoid false alerts.
- **Spontaneous lip curl (flehmen) without a scent trigger:** a discomfort sign.
- **Before foaling:**
  - Restlessness rises from about 2 h before the birth.
  - Sensor alarms gave 30–60 min of warning.
  - **Disturbing the mare lengthened delivery** (5.3 vs 1.6 min), so watch from outside.

## 9. What monitoring studies teach EquiCare

| Lesson | Evidence | What EquiCare does or should do |
|---|---|---|
| Lying is the class video misses most | Lying recall was 63% in a stall CNN (Giannone 2026); about 92% with a temporal model (Liu 2024) | Smooth over time; consider keypoints to separate chest from flat lying |
| Models fall on unseen horses | F about 0.93 dropped to about 0.69 (Martin-Cirera 2024); a model once learned the stall background (Broomé 2019) | Validate on horses the model has never seen |
| Eating time is the most sensitive early signal | Cattle rumination fell 34% and flagged illness 2–3 days before diagnosis | Already measured (time at the hay); add per-horse trend alerts |
| Baselines | No horse study sets a number of days | 7–14 nights (inferred from cattle work); ignore the first 3–4 days after a move; prefer trend rules needing 2+ signals |
| Colic alarms must be behaviour-specific | A simple lying/standing-transition rule gave 18× more false foaling alarms | Use rolling, repeated down-up, pawing and kicking over 30-min windows |
| Commercial colic alarms | NightWatch, Equisense and others have no peer-reviewed validation | — |
| Breathing from video | About ±8 breaths/min error in cattle, best on a lying animal | Validate against hand counts for each horse |

## 10. Still unknown (no published answer)

The behaviour literature has nothing on these:
- How many hours before a natural colic eating, droppings, lying or activity change.
- The hours of the day when colic most often starts.
- Rolls or lie-downs per hour that mean colic.
- A time limit for lying, or for lying flat.
- A weaving rhythm. The "30–90 swings a minute" figure is from a commercial website.
- Urine volume per event.
- A "no manure for X hours" line.
- An eye-temperature fever line.
- Lying and sleep in heat above 30 °C.
- Whether drinking can be seen on stall video.
- Behaviour norms for Indian breeds or Indian Army horses.

Any number EquiCare uses for these is its own choice, marked `ours` in the knowledge base, and should be reviewed with a vet.
